# Terms

A glossary of chess words not covered by a note of their own elsewhere.
Written for this app in its own words.

Sources the facts were checked against: Wikipedia's glossary of chess, the
FIDE Laws of Chess, and the Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings' own description
of its codes.

## En prise
Also: en prise; hanging piece; hanging; undefended piece
French for "in take": a piece that is attacked and can be captured for free
or at a profit. A piece left en prise is "hanging". Leaving pieces hanging is
the most common way games are lost at club level.

## Blunder
Also: blunder; mistake; inaccuracy; what is a blunder
A blunder is a very bad move that throws away material or the game, marked
?? in notation. A mistake (?) is a serious error, and an inaccuracy (?!) a
smaller one. Engines classify moves by how much they drop the evaluation; in
this app's reviews, the same words describe how much a move cost.

## Minor and major pieces
Also: minor piece; major piece; heavy pieces; light pieces
The bishops and knights are the minor pieces; the rooks and queens are the
major (or heavy) pieces. Major pieces can mate a lone king with the help of
their own king; a single minor piece cannot.

## Ranks, files and diagonals
Also: rank; file; diagonal; long diagonal
Ranks are the horizontal rows, numbered 1 to 8 from White's side. Files are
the vertical columns, lettered a to h from White's left. Diagonals are the
slanted lines of same-coloured squares; the two longest, a1-h8 and h1-a8, are
the long diagonals.

## Kingside and queenside
Also: kingside; queenside; wing; flank
The kingside is the half of the board where the kings start (the e- to
h-files, in practice the f-, g- and h-files); the queenside is the other half
(the a- to d-files). Play on either is called play on the wing or flank.

## Ply and half-move
Also: ply; half move; plies
A ply is one move by one player. In chess usage a "move" usually means a
move by each side, so a 40-move game has about 80 plies. Engines and the
fifty-move rule count plies.

## Opening theory
Also: theory; book moves; opening book; main line; sideline
Opening theory is the body of analysed opening lines. Moves within known
theory are "book" moves; the most played line is the main line, others are
sidelines. Some main lines are analysed beyond move 30. Knowing the ideas
matters more than memorising moves, especially below master level.

## Novelty
Also: novelty; theoretical novelty; TN; new move
A move played for the first time in a known position, often prepared at home
with an engine. A strong novelty can surprise an opponent who is out of their
preparation. Marked N or TN in annotations.

## ECO codes
Also: ECO code; Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings; opening codes; A00; B90
The Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings classifies openings into five volumes,
A to E, each with codes 00 to 99: A covers flank openings and some others
such as the Dutch and the Benoni, B semi-open games
other than the French (such as the Sicilian and Caro-Kann), C open games and
the French, D closed games and the Grünfeld, and E Indian defences. For
example, B90 is the Najdorf and C60 the Ruy Lopez.

## Sharp, quiet and positional
Also: sharp position; quiet move; positional player; tactical player
A sharp position is one with concrete tactics and threats, where one mistake
can decide the game. A quiet move makes no direct threat or capture. A
positional player prefers long-term advantages; a tactical player looks for
combinations. Most strong players are comfortable with both.

## Forcing moves
Also: forcing move; checks captures threats
Checks, captures and threats are forcing moves, because they limit the
opponent's replies. When looking for tactics, consider forcing moves first,
for both sides.

## Swindle
Also: swindle; swindling; cheapo
Saving or winning a lost position with a trick the opponent falls for, such
as a stalemate trap or a hidden mate threat. Frank Marshall and Hikaru
Nakamura are famous for their swindles.

## Patzer and woodpusher
Also: patzer; woodpusher; fish; kibitzer; kibitz
Friendly insults for weak players: a patzer or woodpusher plays badly, a
"fish" in online slang. A kibitzer is a spectator who gives unwanted advice
during a game, from the Yiddish word kibitz.

## Simultaneous exhibition
Also: simul; simultaneous exhibition
A strong player plays many opponents at once, walking from board to board and
making one move at each. Grandmasters often play dozens of boards at a time.

## Blindfold chess
Also: blindfold chess; playing without sight of the board
Playing without seeing the board, with moves called out in notation. Strong
players can play several blindfold games at once; record blindfold simuls
have exceeded forty boards.

## Correspondence chess
Also: correspondence chess; postal chess; daily chess
Games played over days or weeks per move, once by post and now online.
Engine use is allowed in official correspondence events today, so the best
correspondence players are expert engine operators.

## Chess problems and studies
Also: chess problem; mate in two; endgame study; composition; helpmate
Chess compositions are created positions with a single solution. A problem
asks for something specific, such as "White to play and mate in two"; a study
looks like a realistic endgame where White must win or draw with precise,
often surprising moves. A helpmate is a problem where both sides cooperate to
mate Black.

## Puzzles
Also: chess puzzles; tactics puzzles; puzzle rush
Puzzles are positions taken from games where one side has a winning tactic
to find. Solving them regularly is the fastest way for most players to
improve, because it trains pattern recognition. Online sites offer endless
puzzle sets and timed modes.

## Draw death
Also: drawish; draw death; too many draws at the top
The worry that top-level chess has become too drawish because of deep
engine preparation. Fischer's Chess960, faster time controls, and rules
against early draw offers are all attempts to keep games decisive.

## Pre-moves and online chess terms
Also: premove; berserk; flag; mouse slip
Online, a premove is a move entered before the opponent has moved, played
instantly if legal: vital in bullet. To flag someone is to win on time; a
mouse slip is an accidental move. Berserk, on Lichess arena tournaments,
halves your own clock for an extra point if you win.

## Theoretical draw
Also: theoretical draw; dead draw; book draw
A position known to be a draw with correct play, such as most rook and pawn
against rook endings with the defending king in front of the pawn, or king
and lone minor piece against king. A "dead draw" has no winning chances for
either side.

## Material and imbalance
Also: imbalance; material imbalance; up material; down material
Material is the total value of a side's pieces. An imbalance is any
difference between the two sides: material (a rook against two minor pieces),
pawn structure, the bishop pair, development, space. Strong players look for
imbalances that favour them and steer the game toward them.

## Grandmaster draw
Also: grandmaster draw; short draw; quick draw
A short, agreed draw with little fighting, between players who are happy to
split the point. Tournaments discourage it with rules against early draw
offers.

## Chess boxing and other variants
Also: chess variants; bughouse; crazyhouse; three check; king of the hill; atomic chess
Many variants exist. Bughouse is played by teams of two on two boards,
passing captured pieces to a partner; crazyhouse is the one-board version
where captured pieces can be dropped back into play. Three-check is won by
giving three checks, King of the Hill by bringing the king to the centre, and
atomic chess makes captures explode. Chess boxing alternates rounds of
chess and boxing.
